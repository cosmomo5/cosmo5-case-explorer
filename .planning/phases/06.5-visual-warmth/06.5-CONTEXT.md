# Phase 6.5: Visual Warmth — Restoring the Observatory Atmosphere

## Why This Phase Exists

Mo tested the app after Phase 6 and the word was: "cold." The v3 rewrite preserved structural correctness (orbital mechanics, state machine, filter logic, layout) but lost the visual warmth that made the original version feel like an inhabited observatory rather than a technical demo.

Three specific things were lost in the rewrite:

### 1. Star Glow Sprites (the biggest loss)

**Original (cosmos.js:446-478):** Every star had two objects — a `MeshBasicMaterial` sphere for the core, and a `SpriteMaterial` glow halo using `AdditiveBlending` at 7x the star's radius. The glow was generated from a per-category canvas texture (`glowTex` function, cosmos.js:296-311) — a 128px radial gradient from center-bright to edge-transparent, colored by category. Stars *emitted light* visually.

**Current (scene-state.ts:126-134):** Stars are bare `MeshStandardMaterial` spheres lit by a 3-light rig. They look like marbles — physically lit objects that reflect rather than emit. There are no glow sprites. No twinkle animation. No per-category color variation in the glow.

**Why it matters:** `MeshStandardMaterial` is for objects that receive light. Stars are objects that produce light. The material choice is categorically wrong for the metaphor.

### 2. Twinkle Animation (the "alive" quality)

**Original (cosmos.js:488, 933-935):** Each glow sprite had a random phase offset. The tick loop oscillated opacity: `0.3 + 0.2 * Math.sin(elapsed * 0.7 + phase)`. Stars breathed.

**Current:** Static opacity. No animation on individual star points. Stars are inert.

### 3. Nebula Warmth (atmospheric depth)

**Original nebula (cosmos.html:53-63):**
- `rgba(4,13,218,.12)` — deep blue at 12%
- `rgba(0,226,224,.07)` — teal at 7%
- `rgba(76,10,138,.14)` — deep purple at 14%
- `rgba(139,63,204,.09)` — violet at 9%

The purple/violet at 9-14% opacity on near-black read as "deep space with emotional color."

**Current nebula (style.css:31-43):**
- `rgba(4,13,218,.12)` — deep blue at 12% (kept)
- `rgba(0,226,224,.07)` — teal at 7% (kept)
- `rgba(232,177,90,.06)` — gold at 6% (new, replaces purple)
- `rgba(232,177,90,.04)` — gold at 4% (new, replaces violet)

Gold at 4-6% opacity on `#0B0C10` is perceptually invisible. The nebula went from "deep space" to "slightly tinted nothing."

## What We're NOT Doing

- No UnrealBloomPass / postprocessing. The original's warmth came entirely from sprite glow + twinkle + colored nebula. Bloom adds complexity, kills mobile, gives a "game engine" look that doesn't match the analog-instrument register.
- No shader-based SDF points. The sprite approach is simpler, battle-tested, and sufficient.
- No fundamental architecture changes. This is a rendering pass — adding sprites alongside existing meshes and adjusting nebula colors.

## Technique: Canvas Texture Sprite Glow (same as original)

Port the `glowTex` pattern from cosmos.js:
1. Generate a 128px canvas with radial gradient (center → edge, category-colored)
2. Cache textures per color
3. Create `THREE.Sprite` with `SpriteMaterial({ map, transparent, opacity, depthWrite: false, blending: THREE.AdditiveBlending })`
4. Scale sprite to ~7x star radius
5. Position sprite at star position (they orbit together)
6. Oscillate opacity in tick loop with per-sprite random phase

## Design Research

The research agent confirmed: the canvas-texture sprite with AdditiveBlending is the industry standard for per-object glow on dark backgrounds in Three.js. No technically superior alternative exists at this scale. The original implementation was already using the correct technique.

## Files Affected

- `src/scene/scene-state.ts` — star creation (add glow sprites), tick loop (add twinkle)
- `src/style.css` — nebula gradient colors (replace gold with warmer alternatives)
- `src/scene/regroup.ts` — star creation during regroup (same sprite pattern)

## Done Criteria

1. Each star has a visible soft glow halo that extends beyond the mesh boundary
2. Stars twinkle — opacity oscillates at different phases per star
3. The nebula background has visible atmospheric depth (not perceptually flat)
4. The overall impression shifts from "technical demo" to "inhabited observatory"
5. Mo opens the app and the first word is NOT "cold"
